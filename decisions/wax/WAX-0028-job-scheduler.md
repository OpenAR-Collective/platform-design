# Wax Design Decision 28: Job Scheduler

## Decision

Wax will provide a Job Scheduler: a store of system-level jobs scheduled to run at a future time, either once or on a recurring cadence. A job targets a batch workflow run or a maintenance routine rather than a single aggregate. Job definitions are administered configuration, and each run of a job is recorded as an execution event in core.event.

## Not To Be Confused With the Pending Event Register

The Job Scheduler is paired with the Pending Event Register in [Wax Design Decision 27](WAX-0027-pending-event-register.md). The Job Scheduler runs system-level jobs that operate over scoped sets of rows or perform maintenance, where the Pending Event Register schedules individual future-dated domain events aimed at a single aggregate. The distinguishing axis is the nature of the work, a system job versus a per-aggregate event, not the cadence. Both subsystems can schedule a one-time future item; only the Job Scheduler also supports recurrence.

## Jobs, One-Time and Recurring

A job definition names a target, either a batch workflow with a runtime scope selection or a maintenance routine, and a schedule. The schedule is either a single future run time or a recurring expression such as nightly or the first day of each month. A recurring job re-arms for its next occurrence when it fires. Job definitions are administered rather than generated by per-account activity, so they number in the dozens rather than the hundreds of thousands. A recurring job is enabled, disabled, or paused rather than cancelled, since it represents a standing instruction; a one-time job that has not yet run can be removed before its run time.

A one-time run is an instant. A recurring expression is evaluated in a named zone of reference, which is the organization's business zone unless the job definition names another, and it names a calendar when it counts business days, as in the first business day of each month. The next occurrence is computed in that zone on every firing, so a job set for 2 a.m. runs at 2 a.m. on the organization's clock through daylight saving transitions. A local time that does not exist on a transition day runs at the first valid moment after it, and a local time that occurs twice runs once.

## Definitions as Configuration, Runs as History

Job definitions are versioned configuration under [Wax Design Decision 21](WAX-0021-configuration-management-and-environment-lifecycle.md) rather than entries in core.event, because a definition is a standing instruction rather than a recorded occurrence. Each firing of a job emits an execution event to core.event, so the audit trail records that the job ran, when, and with what outcome. The definition is configuration; the run is history.

## Firing and Execution

The Job Scheduler shares the wall-clock polling mechanism with the Pending Event Register but uses a separate store and a separate operational view. A worker claims due jobs using SKIP LOCKED, consistent with the coordination model in [Wax Design Decision 24](WAX-0024-high-availability-disaster-recovery-and-distributed-workloads.md), and the claiming instance coordinates that run. When a job fires, it starts its target: a batch workflow run, whose progress is tracked by the batch run tracking in [Wax Design Decision 23](WAX-0023-workflow-automation-engine.md), or a maintenance routine. A recurring job schedules its next occurrence on firing.

## Separate Operational View

Keeping jobs in their own store rather than mixing them into the Pending Event Register keeps both operational views legible. An administrator reviewing the dozen standing system jobs is not searching through hundreds of thousands of pending per-account events to find them, and a reviewer looking at an account's pending events is not seeing system maintenance jobs interleaved with account actions.

## Implementation Phasing

**Wax v2:** Job definitions with one-time and recurring schedules, the polling and firing mechanism, batch workflow run targets, maintenance routine targets, and execution events. The Job Scheduler matures alongside the batch workflow capability in [Wax Design Decision 23](WAX-0023-workflow-automation-engine.md).

**Breaking change risk: LOW. Job definitions are configuration and the run history lives in core.event, so the operational schedule store is rebuildable. Additional job target types and schedule expression forms are additive.**
