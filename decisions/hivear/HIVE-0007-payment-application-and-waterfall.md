# HiveAR Design Decision 7: Payment Application and Waterfall

## Decision

HiveAR will apply each incoming payment or credit to a debt's balance buckets through a configurable waterfall, an ordered priority that determines which buckets a payment pays down and in what order. Application is the distribution step that decides where money lands; the general ledger from [HiveAR Design Decision 6](HIVE-0006-gaap-journal-and-general-ledger.md) is the posting step that books the result. When a debtor owes multiple debts and makes one payment, the payment is first allocated across debts, honoring the consumer's directions and keeping any amount in dispute out of an allocation the consumer did not direct, and then each debt's waterfall runs. Any money remaining after all eligible buckets are satisfied becomes the consumer credit balance from [HiveAR Design Decision 6](HIVE-0006-gaap-journal-and-general-ledger.md).

## Application Decides Where Money Lands; the Ledger Decides How It Books

This decision owns the distribution of a payment across buckets and produces the per-bucket paydown amounts. [HiveAR Design Decision 6](HIVE-0006-gaap-journal-and-general-ledger.md) then posts those amounts, recognizing commission on the debt paid down and splitting it according to each bucket's treatment. The separation is clean: application determines where the money goes, and the ledger determines how that money books. The same engine serves a fresh payment and the application of a held credit balance, because both are debt paydown, which is why a credit applied to another debt flows through this waterfall exactly as a new payment would.

## The Waterfall Is a Configurable Ordered Priority Over Buckets

A waterfall is an ordered list of buckets, and a payment fills them in order until it is exhausted. Because the buckets from [HiveAR Design Decision 5](HIVE-0005-debt-structure-and-balance-composition.md) are already typed, the familiar question of whether to pay off a whole bucket before the next or to pay a component such as interest across everything first collapses into ordering: paying interest before principal is simply ranking the interest buckets ahead of the principal buckets. The waterfall references bucket definitions from the agency registry and resolves against the buckets actually attached to the debt, skipping any that are absent, so a waterfall that names a bucket the debt does not carry passes over it without incident.

Defaults come from modules and overrides from configuration, consistent with the founding tenet. A debt-type module ships a sensible default waterfall for the buckets it contributes, and the agency or a client contract overrides it as versioned configuration under [Wax Design Decision 21](../wax/WAX-0021-configuration-management-and-environment-lifecycle.md). When more than one override applies, the contract takes precedence over the client, the client over the agency, and the agency over the module default. The contract is the finest configured grain, which keeps a bare install functional while letting clients impose the complex application sequences described in [HiveAR Design Decision 5](HIVE-0005-debt-structure-and-balance-composition.md).

## Per-Payment Override at Posting

The configured waterfall is the default path, not a constraint on the payment-taker. Whoever posts a payment, a cashier on the phone with the consumer, a file import, or an API caller, may override the waterfall by specifying explicit bucket amounts for that payment. There is no separate override permission, because the authority to take a payment is the authority to direct it: a payment-taker may at any moment be receiving the consumer's instructions about how to apply it, so the two cannot be separated. An override applies only to the payment that carries it and does not alter the debt's configured waterfall.

An override that would reach an amount in dispute is the one limit on this authority. Payments never apply to a disputed counterpart, so money reaches an amount in dispute only after the amount moves back to its bare bucket, and that move needs the consumer's direction recorded on the command, as the section on directions to pay an amount in dispute describes. The payment-taker's authority to direct a payment does not reach past that rule, and the platform asks the question of every caller, not only of the person at a screen.

## Multiple Debts, Consumer Directions, and Disputed Amounts

When a debtor owes more than one debt and makes a single payment, two rules govern how it is allocated across those debts, and no configuration relaxes either of them. The consumer's allocation directions are honored above any configured default. Money that the consumer did not direct applies only to amounts that are not in dispute, and money reaches an amount in dispute only by the consumer's specific direction. By default a debt with any disputed part receives none of the money that the consumer did not direct, and what cannot be applied becomes the consumer credit balance of [HiveAR Design Decision 6](HIVE-0006-gaap-journal-and-general-ledger.md). An agency setting, off by default, lets that money post to the part of a partly disputed debt that is not in dispute, while a debt whose whole balance is disputed still receives none of it, and no setting lets it reach the disputed part. Disputes arise in every jurisdiction, so both rules are core mechanisms that hold for every debt. What a jurisdiction's law says about them, such as how a debtor may raise a dispute and which debts its rule governs, is Compliance Pack content under [Wax Design Decision 8](../wax/WAX-0008-extensibility-construct-nomenclature.md), and [United States Module Design Decision 4](../modules/united-states/MOD-US-0004-federal-payment-allocation-across-debts.md) describes the United States federal values.

Within those rules, HiveAR offers configured cross-debt allocation for the common case where the consumer gives no specific direction. A payment is posted against a debt or a debt group at any level of the debt's grouping scheme, which the payment-taker or the import names: an import that carries only a debt identifier posts against that debt, and a screen preselects the group the collector has open. The core ships no default level for posting, and agency, client, and contract settings can supply one. One payment can carry several directed parts. Each part names a debt or a group at any level, with an amount or the rest of the payment, and the consumer, the cashier, or the import can direct the parts, including to the debts of more than one client when someone directs them there. A payment still has one currency, and its parts go only to debts in that currency. A part that goes to a group is spread directly across all the debts beneath it by the distribution rule of the group's level under [HiveAR Design Decision 4](HIVE-0004-debt-groups.md), and the rule may use the hierarchy as an ordering key, such as oldest visit first. A rule that spreads money level by level can come later as one more kind of distribution rule. Version 1 provides three distribution rules: an even split, a proportional split by the balance each debt owes, and a waterfall that pays certain debts first and then moves to the next, such as oldest first by list date or a configured priority. Later versions add more as agencies ask for them. An even or a proportional split rounds each share to the currency's minor unit under the rounding convention that the currency's Region Pack supplies, as [Wax Design Decision 34](../wax/WAX-0034-monetary-values-and-currency.md) requires of a rule that rounds. Money that no direction covers goes through a distribution rule too, never reaches an amount in dispute, or by default any debt with a disputed part, and what no rule can place becomes the consumer credit balance of [HiveAR Design Decision 6](HIVE-0006-gaap-journal-and-general-ledger.md). Undirected money stays within the scope of its distribution rule, which defaults to the debts beneath the group the payment is posted against. A cashier can widen that scope to any debts they choose, and agency, client, and contract settings can change the default with the same precedence as the waterfall: the contract over the client, the client over the agency, and the agency over the core default, which is the cautious one. The allocation across debts runs first, and then each debt's bucket waterfall runs on its share.

No pack relaxes either rule. A Compliance Pack may tighten the second one by setting that a debt with any disputed part is treated as disputed in whole for money the consumer did not direct, so that the debt receives none of it, and a pack's value for that setting stays flagged until someone with compliance standing verifies it. The core applies the rule to the disputed amounts alone. A pack may also set how a directed excess is treated, as the section on money that cannot be applied describes. Neither rule covers that setting, because an excess beyond what a debt can take was directed to no other debt, and money spread as undirected is still bound by the second rule.

## A Direction to Pay an Amount in Dispute

An amount in dispute sits in the disputed counterpart of its bucket, and payments never apply there. A consumer who wants to pay such an amount directs it, and the direction does two things in one transaction. It moves the amount back from the counterpart to its bare bucket, as [HiveAR Design Decision 12](HIVE-0012-disputes.md) describes, and the payment then applies to the bare bucket like any other money the consumer directed. The command that carries the explicit bucket amounts names the amount in dispute that the consumer is directing money to, and it states that the consumer directed it. A direction to pay a debt, or a group, that does not name the amount in dispute reaches only the amounts that are not in dispute.

That statement is the recorded consumer direction. It is a field of the command, stored with the event together with who asserted it and the source it came from, such as a call, a file, or an API client. Any caller may assert it, whether a payment-taker at a screen, a file import, or an API client, and the record shows who claimed it. When the statement is missing, the platform refuses the command on an interactive screen with a clear message, and it routes a file or API item to the exceptions queue that [Shared Design Decision 6](../shared/SHARED-0006-file-interface-engine.md) provides for imports. A narrowing or a withdrawal of a dispute that is not part of a payment needs the same statement.

## Edge Cases in Allocation

The allocation across debts has three edge cases, and each has one answer. In them a debt can take at most the balance of the buckets that the money may reach, which for money the consumer did not direct excludes any amount in dispute. When an even split would give a debt more than it can take, that debt is paid in full and the excess is split again across the remaining eligible debts, repeating until the money or the eligible balances are exhausted. When a split does not divide evenly, the odd minor units go one each to the debts with the earliest list dates, and among debts with the same list date by primary key, so that the split is deterministic, which is the rounding convention that [Wax Design Decision 34](../wax/WAX-0034-monetary-values-and-currency.md) requires a rule that rounds to name. When a payment carries explicit bucket amounts for a debt that total less than the payment, that debt receives exactly those amounts, the remainder is shared across the payment's other debts under the configured allocation, and whatever is left becomes a credit.

## Money That Cannot Be Applied

Money that no eligible amount takes becomes a credit on the consumer's credit balance of [HiveAR Design Decision 6](HIVE-0006-gaap-journal-and-general-ledger.md), which belongs to the consumer and to no debt. An undirected payment is allocated across the consumer's debts, and within each debt across the amounts that are not in dispute, by the configured options. What those amounts cannot take becomes a credit. When nothing undisputed is owed, whether because every debt is wholly in dispute or because the consumer has no open debt, the whole payment becomes a credit.

When a consumer directs more to a debt than the debt can take, the excess is not moved to another debt, because the consumer did not direct it there. It becomes a credit. A pack can set that such an excess is treated as undirected money instead, so that it is allocated across the consumer's other debts like any money the consumer did not direct. The excess lies beyond what the consumer's direction reaches, so treating it as undirected money relaxes neither of the two rules. The core's default is the credit, and a pack's value for this setting stays flagged until someone with compliance standing verifies it.

A held credit is applied to a debt through the same allocation and waterfall as a new payment, in the credit's currency. The rules for consumer directions and disputed amounts apply to it unchanged, so a credit that the agency applies without the consumer's direction is undirected money and never reaches an amount in dispute.

## Currency in Application

A payment carries one currency. When it pays a debt, that is the debt's currency and the waterfall runs in it, and a payment that pays no debt becomes a credit in its own currency. The platform does not convert between currencies, because converting money is the work of a bank or a payment provider, as [Wax Design Decision 34](../wax/WAX-0034-monetary-values-and-currency.md) provides. A consumer who wants to pay a US dollar debt in pesos has the agency's payment provider perform the conversion, and the payment reaches the platform as the amount applied in US dollars. A payment whose currency differs from its debt's is rejected, so a bucket is never paid in a currency it does not hold. One payment is never applied to debts of different currencies. An allocation across debts is confined to debts in the payment's currency, and every debt group holds debts of one currency at every level, as [HiveAR Design Decision 4](HIVE-0004-debt-groups.md) provides, so a consumer with debts in two currencies has separate groups for each currency and makes a separate payment for each. A held credit is in the currency of the payment that created it and applies only to debts in that currency. In a single-currency installation every payment and every debt share the one currency, and none of this is visible.

## Payment Arrangements Carry a Persisted Application Directive

A consumer's direction can endure rather than apply only once. When a consumer sets up a payment arrangement and states how the payments should be applied, that instruction persists, so each scheduled payment applies the same way without re-asking. This decision models that persisted application directive, the standing instruction an arrangement payment carries into the waterfall, because honoring it is a continuation of the same consumer-direction rule that governs a single payment. It does not model the arrangement itself, the installment schedule, the amounts and due dates, promise tracking, and broken-arrangement handling, which is a separate decision that will reuse this directive.

## Reversals Unwind the Original Distribution

A returned payment is reversed by backing out the exact distribution the original application produced, both the allocation across debts and the per-bucket paydown within each, rather than by recomputing against the current configuration. Because the original distribution is recorded in the event stream, the reversal is faithful even when the waterfall configuration has changed since the payment posted. The ledger entries reverse in step, as described in [HiveAR Design Decision 6](HIVE-0006-gaap-journal-and-general-ledger.md), and the reversal reduces the next remittance. A reversal also backs out the credit that the payment created, so far as that credit is still held. What a reversal does about credit that has since been applied to a debt or refunded is an open question in [OPEN-QUESTIONS.md](../../OPEN-QUESTIONS.md).

## Scope and Boundaries

This decision covers the distribution of payments and credits across debts and buckets, the configurable waterfall, the per-payment override, the rules for consumer directions and disputed amounts with the recorded consumer direction, the v1 cross-debt allocation options with their edge cases, the money that cannot be applied, and the persisted application directive. The dispute and its events are defined in [HiveAR Design Decision 12](HIVE-0012-disputes.md), and the credit balance record is defined in HiveAR Design Decision 6; this decision decides how money reaches a disputed amount or becomes a credit. It does not set commission rates or the rules that calculate the split, which belong to the client-billing decision and which the ledger consumes. Settlements, where a consumer resolves a debt for less than the full balance, are a complex area with their own decision; this waterfall applies the settlement payment, and the forgiven remainder is a balance adjustment under HiveAR Design Decisions 5 and 6 with no revenue reversal, since it was never collected, and it can reduce an amount in dispute as [HiveAR Design Decision 12](HIVE-0012-disputes.md) describes. The full payment-arrangement definition is likewise its own decision and reuses the directive defined here. A debt's itemization is outside the waterfall: an itemization is static reference detail that is never posted against, as [HiveAR Design Decision 5](HIVE-0005-debt-structure-and-balance-composition.md) describes, and this waterfall operates over a debt's buckets.

## Implementation Phasing

**HiveAR v1 (MVP):** The configurable bucket waterfall with module-supplied defaults and contract-over-client-over-agency overrides. The per-payment bucket-amount override available to any payment-taker, whether cashier, file import, or API, subject on an amount in dispute to the consumer's recorded direction. The rules for consumer directions and disputed amounts: consumer-directed allocation above any default, and no money the consumer did not direct applied to an amount in dispute, or by default to any debt with a disputed part, with the agency setting that lets it reach the undisputed part. The recorded consumer direction, refused on an interactive screen when it is missing and routed to the exceptions queue for a file or API item. Payments posted against a debt or a group at any level, with directed parts that each name a debt or a group at any level. Three distribution rules: an even split, a proportional split by balance owed, and a waterfall such as oldest first by list date or a configured priority, with the edge cases of allocation. The scope of undirected money, defaulting to the debts beneath the group the payment is posted against, with cashier, agency, client, and contract settings that change it. Money that cannot be applied becomes a credit on the consumer's credit balance, and a directed excess is held there by default. Application of held credits through the same waterfall. The persisted application directive that an arrangement payment carries. Reversals that unwind the original distribution.

**HiveAR v2:** Pro-rata distribution across equal-priority buckets within a waterfall tier, with its rounding rule, which rounds each share to the currency's minor unit under the rounding convention that the currency's Region Pack supplies. Additional cross-debt allocation options as agencies request them.

**HiveAR v3+:** Further allocation strategies and sequencing refinements as feedback warrants, all additive to the waterfall model defined here.

**Breaking change risk: LOW. Application consumes the [HiveAR Design Decision 5](HIVE-0005-debt-structure-and-balance-composition.md) buckets and feeds the [HiveAR Design Decision 6](HIVE-0006-gaap-journal-and-general-ledger.md) ledger without changing either, and the waterfall, the allocation options, and the override are additive. The rules for consumer directions and disputed amounts are correctness-critical behavior to get right at the start, since misapplied or wrongly directed payments are a compliance exposure, but the model itself is stable because it rests on the existing bucket and event-sourcing foundations.**

## Implications For Contributors

Application distributes; the ledger posts. This decision produces per-bucket paydown amounts, and [HiveAR Design Decision 6](HIVE-0006-gaap-journal-and-general-ledger.md) books them. No code in the application path may post to the general ledger or compute commission directly.

The rules for consumer directions and disputed amounts are absolute. Consumer allocation directions are honored above any default, and an amount in dispute is never paid from money the consumer did not direct to it. Whether such money may reach the undisputed part of a partly disputed debt is an agency setting, off by default. No configuration overrides them, and a jurisdiction's law reaches them only through its Compliance Pack.

A payment-taker may direct a payment. The right to post a payment includes the right to override the waterfall with explicit bucket amounts, and no separate permission gates it, except that explicit bucket amounts that reach an amount in dispute are accepted only with the consumer's direction recorded on the command.

The consumer's direction to pay an amount in dispute is a recorded statement on the command, stored with who asserted it and the source. Any caller may assert it, and a command without it is refused on an interactive screen and routed to the exceptions queue for a file or API item.

Money that cannot be applied becomes a credit on the consumer's credit balance. A directed excess is never spread to another debt unless a pack sets that it is, and no code spreads it on its own.

Reversals unwind the recorded distribution, never a recomputed one. A reversal backs out the exact allocation and per-bucket amounts the original application produced, read from the event stream.

The waterfall runs in the debt's currency. A payment whose currency differs from its debt's is rejected, because conversion is the work of the bank or payment provider, and no payment is allocated across debts of different currencies.
