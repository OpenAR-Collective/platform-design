# HiveAR Design Decision 7: Payment Application and Waterfall

## Decision

HiveAR will apply each incoming payment or credit to a debt's balance buckets through a configurable waterfall, an ordered priority that determines which buckets a payment pays down and in what order. Application is the distribution step that decides where money lands; the general ledger from [HiveAR Design Decision 6](HIVE-0006-gaap-journal-and-general-ledger.md) is the posting step that books the result. Every payment records its payer as an entity. A payment honors the payer's directions, money that the payer did not direct reaches only debts that the payer is responsible for, and no payment applies to an amount in dispute. When a payment can reach more than one debt, it is first allocated across debts under those rules, and then each debt's waterfall runs. Any money remaining after all eligible buckets are satisfied becomes the payer's credit balance from [HiveAR Design Decision 6](HIVE-0006-gaap-journal-and-general-ledger.md).

## Application Decides Where Money Lands; the Ledger Decides How It Books

This decision owns the distribution of a payment across buckets and produces the per-bucket paydown amounts. [HiveAR Design Decision 6](HIVE-0006-gaap-journal-and-general-ledger.md) then posts those amounts, recognizing commission on the debt paid down and splitting it according to each bucket's treatment. The separation is clean: application determines where the money goes, and the ledger determines how that money books. The same engine serves a fresh payment and the application of a held credit balance, because both are debt paydown, which is why a credit applied to another debt flows through this waterfall exactly as a new payment would.

## The Waterfall Is a Configurable Ordered Priority Over Buckets

A waterfall is an ordered list of buckets, and a payment fills them in order until it is exhausted. Because the buckets from [HiveAR Design Decision 5](HIVE-0005-debt-structure-and-balance-composition.md) are already typed, the familiar question of whether to pay off a whole bucket before the next or to pay a component such as interest across everything first collapses into ordering: paying interest before principal is simply ranking the interest buckets ahead of the principal buckets. The waterfall references bucket definitions from the agency registry and resolves against the buckets actually attached to the debt, skipping any that are absent, so a waterfall that names a bucket the debt does not carry passes over it without incident.

Defaults come from modules and overrides from configuration, consistent with the founding tenet. A debt-type module ships a sensible default waterfall for the buckets it contributes, and the agency or a client contract overrides it as versioned configuration under [Wax Design Decision 21](../wax/WAX-0021-configuration-management-and-environment-lifecycle.md). When more than one override applies, the contract takes precedence over the client, the client over the agency, and the agency over the module default. The contract is the finest configured grain, which keeps a bare install functional while letting clients impose the complex application sequences described in [HiveAR Design Decision 5](HIVE-0005-debt-structure-and-balance-composition.md).

## Per-Payment Override at Posting

The configured waterfall is the default path, not a constraint on the payment-taker. Whoever posts a payment, a cashier on the phone with the payer, a file import, or an API caller, may override the waterfall by specifying explicit bucket amounts for that payment. There is no separate override permission, because the authority to take a payment is the authority to direct it: a payment-taker may at any moment be receiving the payer's instructions about how to apply it, so the two cannot be separated. An override applies only to the payment that carries it and does not alter the debt's configured waterfall.

An amount in dispute is the one limit on this authority. No payment applies to a disputed counterpart by any route, an override or a direction included. An amount in dispute can be paid only after the consumer has directed it back to its bare bucket, a step of its own that takes the amount out of dispute, as the section on paying an amount in dispute describes. The payment-taker's authority to direct a payment does not reach past that rule, and the platform applies it to every caller, not only to the person at a screen.

## Multiple Debts, the Payer's Directions, and Disputed Amounts

Three rules govern where a payment's money may go, whether it can reach one debt or several, and no configuration relaxes any of them. The payer's allocation directions are honored above any configured default. Money that the payer did not direct reaches only debts that the payer is responsible for, the debts on which the payer's entity holds a role that [HiveAR Design Decision 1](HIVE-0001-entity-data-model-person-business-and-entity-relationships.md) flags as carrying financial liability. When the payer is responsible for none of the debts in scope, the debt or group that the payment is posted against counts as the payer's direction, as the section on the payer describes. No payment applies to an amount in dispute, by any route: a consumer who wants to pay a disputed amount first directs it back out of dispute, as the section on paying an amount in dispute describes. By default a debt with any disputed part receives none of the money that the payer did not direct, and what cannot be applied becomes the payer's credit balance of [HiveAR Design Decision 6](HIVE-0006-gaap-journal-and-general-ledger.md). An agency setting, off by default, lets that money post to the part of a partly disputed debt that is not in dispute, while a debt whose whole balance is disputed still receives none of it, and no setting lets it reach the disputed part. Disputes arise in every jurisdiction, so these rules are core mechanisms that hold for every debt. What a jurisdiction's law says about them, such as how a debtor may raise a dispute and which debts its rule governs, is Compliance Pack content under [Wax Design Decision 8](../wax/WAX-0008-extensibility-construct-nomenclature.md), and [United States Module Design Decision 4](../modules/united-states/MOD-US-0004-federal-payment-allocation-across-debts.md) describes the United States federal values.

Within those rules, HiveAR offers configured cross-debt allocation for the common case where the payer gives no specific direction. A payment is posted against a debt or a debt group at any level of the debt's grouping scheme, which the payment-taker or the import names: an import that carries only a debt identifier posts against that debt, and a screen preselects the group the collector has open. The core ships no default level for posting, and agency, client, and contract settings can supply one. One payment can carry several directed parts. Each part names a debt or a group at any level, with an amount or the rest of the payment, and the payer, the cashier, or the import can direct the parts, including to the debts of more than one client when someone directs them there. A payment still has one currency, and its parts go only to debts in that currency. A part that goes to a group is spread directly across all the debts beneath it by the distribution rule of the group's level under [HiveAR Design Decision 4](HIVE-0004-debt-groups.md), and the rule may use the hierarchy as an ordering key, such as oldest visit first. A rule that spreads money level by level can come later as one more kind of distribution rule. Version 1 provides three distribution rules: an even split, a proportional split by the balance each debt owes, and a waterfall that pays certain debts first and then moves to the next, such as oldest first by list date or a configured priority. Later versions add more as agencies ask for them. An even or a proportional split rounds each share to the currency's minor unit under the rounding convention that the currency's Region Pack supplies, as [Wax Design Decision 34](../wax/WAX-0034-monetary-values-and-currency.md) requires of a rule that rounds. Money that no direction covers goes through a distribution rule too, reaches only debts that the payer is responsible for, never reaches an amount in dispute, or by default any debt with a disputed part, and what no rule can place becomes the payer's credit balance of [HiveAR Design Decision 6](HIVE-0006-gaap-journal-and-general-ledger.md). Undirected money stays within the scope of its distribution rule, which defaults to the debts beneath the group the payment is posted against. A cashier can widen that scope to any debts they choose that the payer is responsible for, and agency, client, and contract settings can change the default with the same precedence as the waterfall: the contract over the client, the client over the agency, and the agency over the core default, which is the cautious one. The allocation across debts runs first, and then each debt's bucket waterfall runs on its share.

## The Payer

Every payment records its payer, the party whose money it is, as an entity of [HiveAR Design Decision 1](HIVE-0001-entity-data-model-person-business-and-entity-relationships.md), and a payer who is not on file is added as an entity from the payment, with the minimum that a refund needs. The payer may be the debtor, a co-signer, a guarantor, a relative, an insurer, or anyone else, and paying needs no authority over the debt; whether a person may also discuss a debt is a question of representation and authorization, which this decision does not decide and which is never a condition of paying. The payer's directions say which debts the money pays, and money that the payer does not direct reaches only the debts that the payer is responsible for. When the payer is responsible for none of the debts in the payment's scope, the debt or group that the payment is posted against counts as the payer's direction, so the money reaches that target through the distribution rule within it. For example, a payer who directs a payment to the two debts that one co-signer guaranteed reaches only those debts. An excess becomes the payer's credit, which belongs to the payer and is refunded only to the payer. The agency may later apply it to those two debts without a new direction, and it never moves to another co-signer's debts without the payer's direction.

## Paying an Amount in Dispute

An amount in dispute sits in the disputed counterpart of its bucket, and no payment applies there by any route, a direction or an override included. A consumer who wants to pay a disputed amount first directs that the amount move back from the counterpart to its bare bucket, which takes it out of dispute and narrows the dispute, as [HiveAR Design Decision 12](HIVE-0012-disputes.md) describes. That move is a command of its own and never part of a payment, and the amount is then paid like any other amount. A direction to pay that names a debt or a group, and not a move back, reaches only the amounts that are not in dispute. Paying an amount while its dispute stays open, so that a payment made under protest could be refunded once the dispute is resolved, is not offered in v1 and is an open question in [OPEN-QUESTIONS.md](../../OPEN-QUESTIONS.md).

The consumer's direction to move an amount back is a statement recorded on the move-back command, stored with the event together with who asserted it and the source it came from, such as a call, a file, or an API client. Any caller may assert it, and the record shows who claimed it. A payment whose explicit bucket amounts aim at an amount that is still in dispute is handled by where it comes from. On an interactive screen it is refused with a clear message, and an API call is refused the same way. A payment in a file is posted, because its deposit has to balance, and the part aimed at the amount still in dispute is held as the payer's credit until the consumer's direction to move the amount back is recorded, after which it can be applied there at the payer's direction. A narrowing or a withdrawal of a dispute needs the same recorded direction.

## Edge Cases in Allocation

The distribution rules across debts have three edge cases, and each has one answer. In them a debt can take at most the balance of the buckets that the money may reach, which excludes any amount in dispute and, for money the payer did not direct, any debt the payer is not responsible for and, by default, the whole of a debt with a disputed part. When an even or a proportional split would give a debt more than it can take, that debt is paid what it can take and the excess is split again by the same rule across the remaining eligible debts, repeating until the money or the eligible balances are exhausted. When the shares of a split, each rounded as the section on multiple debts describes, do not add up to the amount being split, the difference is settled one minor unit at a time on the eligible debts in order of list date, oldest first, and among debts with the same list date by primary key, so that the split is deterministic and its shares add up to the amount. When a payment carries explicit bucket amounts for a debt that total less than the payment, that debt receives exactly those amounts, the remainder goes where the payment's other directed parts send it, and what no direction covers is spread by the distribution rule across the other debts within its scope, under the rules for money that no direction covers. Whatever no rule can place becomes the payer's credit.

## Money That Cannot Be Applied

Money that no eligible amount takes becomes a credit on the payer's credit balance of [HiveAR Design Decision 6](HIVE-0006-gaap-journal-and-general-ledger.md), which belongs to the payer and to no debt. Money that no direction covers reaches only the amounts that the section on multiple debts allows within the scope of its distribution rule, and what those amounts cannot take becomes the payer's credit. When it can reach nothing, such as when every debt in its scope is in dispute or no debt is open, all of it becomes the payer's credit. A refund of a credit goes to the payer. The agency may apply a credit, without a new direction, to the debt or debts that the payer directed the money to, and any other application of a credit takes the payer's direction.

When a directed part gives a debt or a group more than it can take, the excess is not moved to a debt that the part does not name, because the payer did not direct it there. It becomes the payer's credit. An agency setting, off by default, treats such an excess as money that no direction covers instead, so that it is spread by the distribution rule within its scope, and only to debts that the payer is responsible for, like any money that no direction covers. The excess lies beyond what the payer's direction reaches, so treating it as undirected money relaxes none of the rules above. A Compliance Pack can publish its own value for the setting, which stays flagged until someone with compliance standing verifies it, and the platform shows an agency value that is looser than its pack's, as it does for the setting on partly disputed debts.

A held credit is applied to a debt through the same distribution rules and waterfall as a new payment and in the credit's currency, by the agency to the debts that the payer directed the money to or at the payer's direction to any other debt, and the rules for disputed amounts apply to it unchanged.

## Currency in Application

A payment carries one currency. When it pays a debt, that is the debt's currency and the waterfall runs in it, and a payment that pays no debt becomes the payer's credit in its own currency. The platform does not convert between currencies, because converting money is the work of a bank or a payment provider, as [Wax Design Decision 34](../wax/WAX-0034-monetary-values-and-currency.md) provides. A consumer who wants to pay a US dollar debt in pesos has the agency's payment provider perform the conversion, and the payment reaches the platform as the amount applied in US dollars. A payment whose currency differs from its debt's is rejected, so a bucket is never paid in a currency it does not hold. One payment is never applied to debts of different currencies. An allocation across debts is confined to debts in the payment's currency, and every debt group holds debts of one currency at every level, as [HiveAR Design Decision 4](HIVE-0004-debt-groups.md) provides, so a consumer with debts in two currencies has separate groups for each currency and makes a separate payment for each. A held credit is in the currency of the payment that created it and applies only to debts in that currency. In a single-currency installation every payment and every debt share the one currency, and none of this is visible.

## Payment Arrangements Carry a Persisted Application Directive

A payer's direction can endure rather than apply only once. When a consumer sets up a payment arrangement and states how the payments should be applied, that instruction persists, so each scheduled payment applies the same way without re-asking. This decision models that persisted application directive, the standing instruction an arrangement payment carries into the waterfall, because honoring it is a continuation of the same rule on the payer's directions that governs a single payment. It does not model the arrangement itself, the installment schedule, the amounts and due dates, promise tracking, and broken-arrangement handling, which is a separate decision that will reuse this directive.

## Reversals Unwind the Original Distribution

A returned payment is reversed by backing out the exact distribution the original application produced, both the allocation across debts and the per-bucket paydown within each, rather than by recomputing against the current configuration. Because the original distribution is recorded in the event stream, the reversal is faithful even when the waterfall configuration has changed since the payment posted. The ledger entries reverse in step, as described in [HiveAR Design Decision 6](HIVE-0006-gaap-journal-and-general-ledger.md), and the reversal reduces the next remittance. A reversal also backs out the credit that the payment created, so far as that credit is still held. What a reversal does about credit that has since been applied to a debt or refunded is an open question in [OPEN-QUESTIONS.md](../../OPEN-QUESTIONS.md).

## Scope and Boundaries

This decision covers the distribution of payments and credits across debts and buckets, the configurable waterfall, the per-payment override, the payer of each payment, the rules for the payer's directions and for disputed amounts with the consumer's recorded direction to move an amount out of dispute, the v1 distribution rules across debts with their edge cases, the money that cannot be applied, and the persisted application directive. The dispute and its events are defined in [HiveAR Design Decision 12](HIVE-0012-disputes.md), and the credit balance record is defined in HiveAR Design Decision 6; this decision decides when a disputed amount can be paid and how money becomes the payer's credit. It does not set commission rates or the rules that calculate the split, which belong to the client-billing decision and which the ledger consumes. Settlements, where a consumer resolves a debt for less than the full balance, are a complex area with their own decision; this waterfall applies the settlement payment, and the forgiven remainder is a balance adjustment under HiveAR Design Decisions 5 and 6 with no revenue reversal, since it was never collected, and it can reduce an amount in dispute as [HiveAR Design Decision 12](HIVE-0012-disputes.md) describes. The full payment-arrangement definition is likewise its own decision and reuses the directive defined here. A debt's itemization is outside the waterfall: an itemization is static reference detail that is never posted against, as [HiveAR Design Decision 5](HIVE-0005-debt-structure-and-balance-composition.md) describes, and this waterfall operates over a debt's buckets.

## Implementation Phasing

**HiveAR v1 (MVP):** The configurable bucket waterfall with module-supplied defaults and contract-over-client-over-agency overrides. The payer of every payment recorded as an entity, added from the payment when not on file. The per-payment bucket-amount override available to any payment-taker, whether cashier, file import, or API, which never reaches an amount in dispute. The rules for the payer's directions and for disputed amounts: payer-directed allocation above any default, money the payer did not direct reaching only debts the payer is responsible for, with the debt or group a payment is posted against counting as the payer's direction when the payer is responsible for none of the debts in scope, no payment applied to an amount in dispute by any route, and by default no undirected money on a debt with a disputed part, with the agency setting that lets it reach the undisputed part. The consumer's recorded direction to move an amount out of dispute, as a command of its own, with a payment aimed at an amount still in dispute refused on a screen or through the API and posted from a file with that part held as the payer's credit. Payments posted against a debt or a group at any level, with directed parts that each name a debt or a group at any level. Three distribution rules: an even split, a proportional split by balance owed, and a waterfall such as oldest first by list date or a configured priority, with the edge cases of allocation. The scope of undirected money, defaulting to the debts beneath the group the payment is posted against, with cashier, agency, client, and contract settings that change it. Money that cannot be applied becomes the payer's credit, and a directed excess is held there unless the agency's setting treats it as undirected money. Application of held credits through the same waterfall, by the agency to the debts the payer directed the money to and at the payer's direction to any other debt. The persisted application directive that an arrangement payment carries. Reversals that unwind the original distribution.

**HiveAR v2:** Pro-rata distribution across equal-priority buckets within a waterfall tier, with its rounding rule, which rounds each share to the currency's minor unit under the rounding convention that the currency's Region Pack supplies. Additional cross-debt allocation options as agencies request them.

**HiveAR v3+:** Further allocation strategies and sequencing refinements as feedback warrants, all additive to the waterfall model defined here.

**Breaking change risk: LOW. Application consumes the [HiveAR Design Decision 5](HIVE-0005-debt-structure-and-balance-composition.md) buckets and feeds the [HiveAR Design Decision 6](HIVE-0006-gaap-journal-and-general-ledger.md) ledger without changing either, and the waterfall, the allocation options, and the override are additive. The rules for the payer's directions and for disputed amounts are correctness-critical behavior to get right at the start, since misapplied or wrongly directed payments are a compliance exposure, but the model itself is stable because it rests on the existing bucket and event-sourcing foundations.**

## Implications For Contributors

Application distributes; the ledger posts. This decision produces per-bucket paydown amounts, and [HiveAR Design Decision 6](HIVE-0006-gaap-journal-and-general-ledger.md) books them. No code in the application path may post to the general ledger or compute commission directly.

The rules for the payer's directions and for disputed amounts are absolute. The payer's allocation directions are honored above any default, money the payer did not direct reaches only debts the payer is responsible for, and no payment applies to an amount in dispute by any route. When the payer is responsible for none of the debts in scope, the debt or group the payment is posted against counts as the payer's direction. Whether undirected money may reach the undisputed part of a partly disputed debt is an agency setting, off by default. No configuration overrides them, and a jurisdiction's law reaches them only through its Compliance Pack.

A payment-taker may direct a payment. The right to post a payment includes the right to override the waterfall with explicit bucket amounts, and no separate permission gates it. Explicit bucket amounts never reach an amount in dispute: the amount is paid only after the consumer's recorded direction has moved it back, as a command of its own.

The consumer's direction to move an amount out of dispute is a recorded statement on its command, stored with who asserted it and the source. A payment aimed at an amount still in dispute is refused on a screen or through the API, and a payment in a file is posted with that part held as the payer's credit.

Every payment records its payer as an entity. Money that cannot be applied becomes the payer's credit, which is refunded to the payer, applied by the agency without a new direction only to the debts the payer directed the money to, and applied anywhere else only at the payer's direction. A directed excess is never spread to another debt unless the agency's setting treats it as undirected money, and no code spreads it on its own.

Reversals unwind the recorded distribution, never a recomputed one. A reversal backs out the exact allocation and per-bucket amounts the original application produced, read from the event stream.

The waterfall runs in the debt's currency. A payment whose currency differs from its debt's is rejected, because conversion is the work of the bank or payment provider, and no payment is allocated across debts of different currencies.
